\subsection{How transferred evidence changes experimental decisions}
\label{sec:case_studies}

Aggregate metrics do not reveal how source knowledge changes a sequential
experimental trajectory. We therefore audited four representative decisions
from the frozen online runs: one molecular-property task, two materials tasks,
and one wet-lab reaction-optimization task. These cases were selected after the
runs to illustrate decision mechanisms, and are not used for statistical
inference. At every decision, the language model could inspect source evidence,
the revealed target history, and target-only Gaussian-process (GP) predictions.
The outcome of every unexecuted target candidate remained hidden until that
candidate was selected.

\paragraph{Molecular property: source evidence justified an early GP override.}
For FreeSolv $\rightarrow$ Lipophilicity, the three initial target observations
gave a best value of 67.25. The target-only GP ranked
\texttt{lipo\_2256} first in the first online round. The LLM proposer and critic
instead selected \texttt{lipo\_0635}, which was fourth in the GP ranking but had
nearly identical expected improvement to the GP default. The decision cited a
specific transfer hypothesis: relative to hydration free energy, lipophilicity
should favour a more halogenated and less polar aromatic structure. Executing
the candidate revealed 86.875, improving the best observed value by 19.625 in
the first round. The online trajectory finished with a best-so-far AUC of
89.575 and a final best of 89.875, compared with 83.250 and 85.000 for the
same-initial target-only GP. The gain is therefore traceable to an executable
override rather than to a different initial target set.

\paragraph{Materials: the LLM translated a physical relation into an earlier hit.}
For experimental band gap $\rightarrow$ dielectric response, the initial target
best was 24.2902. The transferred hypothesis used the inverse relation between
band gap and polarizability to favour heavy, polarizable chalcogenides over the
wide-gap oxides and halides prominent in the source task. In round one, the LLM
selected the tellurium candidate
\texttt{real\_matbench\_dielectric\_04424}. It was fifth in the target-only GP
ranking, but consistent with both the source-derived physical hypothesis and
the limited target observations. Its hidden target value was 59.5114. CARE 2.0
therefore reached the trajectory maximum immediately, whereas the comparator
reached the same final value later. The final-best difference was zero, but the
best-so-far AUC increased from 52.7663 to 59.5114
($\Delta$AUC=$+6.7452$). This distinction matters experimentally: transfer did
not change the best condition available in the pool; it reduced the number of
rounds required to find it.

\paragraph{Wet-lab chemistry: transferred catalyst--temperature structure improved the final yield.}
For Reizman Suzuki cases 1--3 $\rightarrow$ case 4, the source campaigns
supported a high-temperature catalyst hypothesis, with temperature showing the
largest recorded source correlation (0.647). The initial target set already
contained a 94.1-yield condition using P1--L1 at 110\,$^\circ$C. The LLM used
this agreement between source and target evidence to keep high-temperature
catalyst variants in the candidate menu while still comparing each proposal
against the target-only GP. In round three it selected
\texttt{reizman\_suzuki\_case4\_086} (GP rank 3), improving the best yield to
94.5. In round nine it selected
\texttt{reizman\_suzuki\_case4\_059}, which revealed 99.8. The same-initial
target-only GP finished at 94.1. CARE 2.0 consequently achieved
$\Delta$AUC=$+1.380$ and $\Delta$Final=$+5.700$. This case is weaker as an
early-discovery example than the first two, but it shows that source evidence
can remain useful after several target observations and can change the final
condition found.

\paragraph{Materials abstention: rejecting a source relation can itself be useful.}
For phonon peak $\rightarrow$ bulk modulus, the controller did not treat two
different material properties as directly interchangeable. It set
\texttt{continue\_source\_transfer=false} in all ten online rounds after finding
that the source evidence did not support a reliable phonon-to-modulus ranking.
The LLM nevertheless used composition semantics and the revealed target history
to override the GP rank-one candidate in four rounds. It selected
\texttt{Lu4S6} in round nine and \texttt{O8Pt6} in round ten, reaching a final
normalized score of 77.6127. The same-initial target GP finished at 73.2886.
The resulting $\Delta$AUC=$+3.2404$ and $\Delta$Final=$+4.3241$ therefore support
a narrower mechanism: evidence-based abstention prevented unsupported direct
transfer, while semantic target adaptation still changed the search. They do
not show that phonon outcomes directly transfer to bulk-modulus outcomes.

\paragraph{Outcome-scale audit.}
The same materials trace revealed that the model sometimes described the
normalized bulk-modulus score as GPa. In fact, the replay score is
$100\log_{10}(K_{\mathrm{VRH}}\,[\mathrm{GPa}])/3$ and is dimensionless. This
error did not alter the hidden data or selected candidate, but it weakens the
scientific explanation. The subsequent frozen development protocol therefore
adds an explicit outcome contract containing the raw quantity, raw unit, score
transformation, and optimization direction. It does not expose any unrevealed
target value or change the experiment budget.
In the pre-amendment trace, a deterministic audit found five instances in
which a numeric replay score was coupled to the raw unit GPa. The post-amendment
trajectory contained zero such couplings across 20 parsed proposer and critic
responses. Its online trajectory remained positive relative to the same-initial
target-only GP ($\Delta$AUC=$+3.0825$, $\Delta$Final=$+2.7458$). Because the two
traces are independent stochastic calls, their performance difference is not
used to estimate the effect of the prompt repair; the audit supports only the
narrow claim that this declared unit-error mode was absent after the contract.

\paragraph{Matched classical-baseline stress test.}
We replayed target-only GP-UCB, RGPE, and multitask GP from the exact initial
candidate IDs used by three context-preserving LLM trajectories, with the same
candidate pools and ten target reveals. The rebuilt target-only GP reproduced
the stored online-runner metrics exactly. Relative to the strongest realized
baseline by AUC, the LLM gained $+6.325$ on FreeSolv $\rightarrow$
Lipophilicity and $+1.922$ on phonon peak $\rightarrow$ bulk modulus, but lost
$1.960$ on Reizman cases 1--3 $\rightarrow$ case 4. In that failed route,
multisource ICM-BMA reached a final yield of 99.8 and AUC of 96.38, compared
with 94.5 and 94.42 for the online LLM. The equal-route mean against the
strongest realized baseline was $+2.0956$, with two wins in three routes. This
is a post-hoc single-trajectory stress test, not a calibration-selected or
confirmatory comparison; it identifies both where the LLM survives stronger
transfer-BO baselines and where a classical model remains superior.

\paragraph{Complete route inventory and calibration-gated authority.}
We next replayed every completed route in the frozen 11-route panel from the
exact online-LLM initial candidates against target-only GP-UCB, RGPE, and
multitask GP under the same candidate pool and ten-reveal budget. Relative to
target-only GP, the online LLM achieved an equal-route mean AUC difference of
$+1.753$ (route-bootstrap 95\% interval $[+0.415,+3.315]$), with six wins, two
ties, and three losses. Relative to an analysis-time rule-fixed RGPE
comparator, the mean was $+3.229$ but the interval crossed zero
($[-1.844,+10.141]$; six wins, one tie, four losses). Against the strongest
realized baseline selected post hoc within each route, the mean was $-0.550$
with three wins, one tie, and seven losses. We therefore do not interpret the
online LLM as a uniformly stronger optimizer.

To expose route-selection effects, we also audited all 17 distinct online-LLM
routes in the repository, including six earlier null or negative development
cases. The complete retrospective inventory yielded $+0.762$ AUC versus target
GP (95\% interval $[-0.501,+2.091]$; eight wins, four ties, five losses) and
$+0.987$ versus rule-fixed RGPE (95\% interval $[-2.901,+5.960]$; eight wins,
two ties, seven losses). These results are weaker than the frozen 11-route
panel and are reported to prevent selective route presentation.

Finally, we evaluated a prediction-error gate after three LLM-guided target
reveals. The gate scores only asserted LLM expected outcomes, selects its error
threshold on the other ten routes in a leave-one-route-out analysis, and hands
control to target-only GP when the held-out trajectory is poorly calibrated or
the LLM explicitly abandons its hypothesis. GP continues from the accumulated
observations rather than restarting. The gated controller increased the mean
AUC difference versus target GP from $+1.753$ to $+2.036$ and reduced the loss
count from three routes to two (95\% interval $[+0.425,+3.901]$; five wins,
four ties, two losses). The mean change versus the original LLM was only
$+0.283$ and its interval crossed zero. This is retrospective evidence that
calibration can reduce negative-transfer risk, not prospective evidence of
universal superiority.

We then applied one global threshold of 5 to every route, matching the rule
frozen for future execution rather than selecting a route-specific threshold.
This fixed rule switched to target-only GP on ten of eleven retrospective
trajectories. The mean AUC difference versus target GP was $+2.135$
(route-bootstrap 95\% interval $[+0.515,+4.001]$; six wins, three ties, and two
losses). The mean change relative to the ungated LLM was $+0.381$ with an
interval crossing zero ($[-0.371,+1.432]$), and the gated controller remained
$-0.168$ below the strongest realized route-wise baseline on average. Thus the
fixed replay supports an executable negative-transfer control but not general
baseline superiority.

The gate is now implemented inside the online controller. Once triggered, it
issues no later LLM requests and lets target-only GP-UCB select the remaining
candidates from all observations accumulated through reveal three. Before new
calls, we froze an 11-route by 30-trajectory protocol with the same gate round,
global threshold, hard-abstention conditions, route list, target budget, and
analysis rule. Confirmatory inference is disabled until all 330 trajectories
complete. Because the routes and threshold were informed by the earlier audit,
this protocol estimates prospective trajectory repeatability of the fixed
controller but does not constitute independent external-route validation.
On the saved trajectories, the fixed gate counterfactually replaces 70 later
LLM-guided rounds, equivalent to 140 nominal proposer/critic calls. We report
this as an auditable replay quantity rather than as already realized API
savings.

\paragraph{Repeated calls expose trajectory-level uncertainty.}
The frozen development run for experimental band gap $\rightarrow$ Materials
Project band gap produced $\Delta$AUC=$-2.4464$: the LLM-guided trajectory did
not improve its initial best, while the target-only GP eventually found the
100.0-valued candidate. The first trajectory under the subsequently frozen
repetition protocol produced $\Delta$AUC=$+2.4464$ and also reached 100.0 in
round nine. Across all eleven paired routes, four strictly reversed sign, even
though both batches retained the same aggregate count of six positive, two
tied, and three negative routes. The mean absolute paired AUC change was
1.0479. Because the early calls predate the confirmation lock, this comparison
is a descriptive stochasticity audit rather than a repeated-measures test. It
nevertheless shows that a single stochastic LLM trajectory is not sufficient
to label a source--target pair as transferable or non-transferable. We
therefore treat the current route-level effects as descriptive and
pre-specified a 30-trajectory-per-route confirmation protocol.
At the time of writing, 11 of 330 trajectories have completed; the equal-route
mean $\Delta$AUC is $+1.7532$, with six positive, two tied, and three negative
routes. The first trajectory under an independent v2 lock repeated the Reizman
Suzuki route and remained positive, but with a smaller effect
($\Delta$AUC=$+0.320$, $\Delta$Final=$+0.400$). No confidence interval or
confirmatory claim is reported until the frozen protocol is complete. API
account-limit failures are retained separately and are not counted as
scientific outcomes.

To test whether the controller depends on a particular foundation model, we
also froze an independent GLM-5.3 robustness protocol over the same eleven
routes, initial observations, reveal budget, and target-only GP comparator. It
declares 30 trajectories per route and is analyzed separately from Opus; route
direction agreement is a secondary endpoint rather than a criterion chosen
after observing the results. The first GLM pilot was stopped by provider rate
limits before a complete trajectory formed. A frozen low-frequency amendment
then reached valid scientific decisions but exposed a provider-specific JSON
envelope in a critic response, which the strict validator recorded as a
terminal model-format failure. A subsequent compatibility protocol removes
only a sole top-level \texttt{answer} object before applying the unchanged
scientific schema. Its first execution then exhausted eight in-place HTTP
retries over approximately twelve minutes without a usable response. Earlier
failures remain unchanged, no GLM trajectory is complete, and no GLM effect
estimate is reported.

Together, the audited cases support a specific and limited conclusion. CARE
2.0 can turn source evidence into an explicit, falsifiable hypothesis and use
that hypothesis to deviate from a target-only acquisition policy. Such
deviations can improve either discovery speed or the final condition found.
The repeated-call audit also shows why calibration and stopping rules are part
of the scientific problem: plausible reasoning does not guarantee a stable
trajectory-level benefit.
